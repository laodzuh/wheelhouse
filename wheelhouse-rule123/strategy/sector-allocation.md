# Sector Allocation

*Diversification discipline. The philosophy here is anti-concentration, not
forced diversity — buy what's good, just don't pile too deep into any one
sector.*

## Sector universe

Everything not structurally excluded in
[wheel-rules.md](wheel-rules.md). No positive sector targets — the universe
is a permission set, not a shopping list.

## General sector cap

- **Max exposure per sector:** 30% of total portfolio capital across
  both open CSPs and assigned shares within that sector.
- **Self-enforcement:** if a sector hits or exceeds 30%, no new
  positions in that sector until exposure drops below the cap. No
  special rebalancing trigger — the cap is the rule.
- **No minimums.** If I have no good ideas in a sector, the book
  stays empty there. I won't force exposure for diversity's sake.

Specific sectors may have tighter caps applied via the *Current macro
view* section below. Tighter caps stack on top of (not in addition to)
the general 30%.

## Current macro view

*This section is cyclical, not structural. It captures my current 1-3 year
stance and is meant to be revisited regularly. When a concern resolves, the
override comes off.*

**Last reviewed:** 2026-05-09

### AI-adjacent / Tech sector

I'm afraid of an AI bubble but don't want to be left out if we're not close
to a pop. Encoded as a layered caution rather than an exit:

- **Tech sector cap tightened to 20%** of total capital (overrides the
  general 30% cap above). Same self-enforcement: at-cap means no new
  Tech positions until exposure drops.
- **AI-adjacent positions get a strike-conservatism overlay.** Within
  Tech (and Comm Services names with heavy AI exposure like META, GOOGL),
  push delta toward the conservative end of the 0.15–0.30 range (closer
  to 0.15) and prefer strikes near support over chasing premium.
- The AI exposure bleeds across GICS sectors (Tech, Comm Services,
  Industrials with data-center buildouts, Utilities with power demand).
  The cap is sector-based for mechanical enforcement, but the
  strike-conservatism overlay applies wherever the AI exposure shows up,
  not just inside Tech.

### SaaS specifically

I'm separately nervous about SaaS — high multiples that haven't fully
reset, AI as a real disruption threat to horizontal SaaS workflow,
customer growth slowing as cloud spend is scrutinized.

- **Profitable SaaS only:** positive cash flow, no GAAP-loss names. Even
  inside the Tech cap, GAAP-loss SaaS is off the table until the
  category resets further.
